This Thursday, I get to celebrate Thanksgiving as a free man.

For a lot of people, that sentence would not mean much. For me, it is the whole story. This is my first Thanksgiving free after 18 years of incarceration, and it is a day I honestly never thought I would see.

So this week, I want to tell you where that gratitude came from. Not because my story is special, but because some of you are sitting right where I once sat, and I want to walk a little of this road with you.

## The day the walls went up

When I went to prison, I was sentenced to 17 and a half to 50 years.

Fifty years. Say that number slowly and feel the weight of it. When I walked into quarantine, I believed I was never going to go home. I thought that was it. That this was going to be the rest of my existence.

Maybe you know that feeling, even if you have never been behind a fence. Maybe your prison is an addiction, a diagnosis, a marriage that came apart, a debt you can't climb out of, or a past you can't seem to outrun. It is that moment when the walls feel permanent and the future goes quiet. When we stop asking what's next, because we are sure there isn't anything next.

That is where I started. Gratitude was the furthest thing from my mind.

## Hope came in small pieces

Nothing changed overnight. Hope did not kick the door down. It slipped in quietly, a little at a time.

Friends came along. I gained my education. I got involved. And slowly, almost without noticing, I started to gain a little bit of hope.

We don't talk about that enough. We wait for the big rescue, the one moment when everything turns around. But most of the time, hope shows up looking ordinary. A friend who sits with us. Something we finish. A reason to get up in the morning. Small things, laid one on top of another like bricks, until one day we look down and realize we are standing on something solid.

## The foundation that held

What I was standing on, in the end, was Jesus Christ. He became my foundation.

A friend of mine once told me something I have never forgotten. If I was looking for love outside of myself, I was never going to find it. The only one who would ever love me the way I truly wanted to be loved was God.

Think about how much of life we spend looking for that kind of love somewhere else. In people. In approval. In success, in the next relationship, in the next thing. We pour ourselves out trying to fill a space that was only ever shaped for Him.

And here is the part that still amazes me. That love was not waiting for me on the other side of the gate. It did not wait for my life to look good again. It met me right there, with a long sentence still ahead of me and a lot of questions I couldn't answer.

## Why this Thanksgiving is different

Gratitude means something different when you once believed you would never go home.

This year, I am thankful for the simple things most people never think about. Walking out a door when I choose. Deciding how I spend my day. Being here at all, for a holiday I was sure I had lost for good.

But looking back, I can see that the freedom I am thankful for didn't start the day I walked out. The walls came down on the inside first. The hope, the friends, the education, the faith. That was the real door opening. The gate just caught up later.

## If you are still behind the walls

Maybe you are reading this with walls of your own around you this Thanksgiving. Maybe it feels like the door will never open. I know that place. I lived in it for a long time.

I am not going to tell you to smile and just be grateful. That is not how it worked for me. What I can tell you is that hope does not need the whole way out to be visible before it starts. It can start with one friend, one small win, one honest prayer. And the love you have been looking for is not out there somewhere, just out of reach. It has been closer than you think the whole time.

This Thursday, I will be giving thanks for a day I never thought I would see. My prayer is that one day, maybe sooner than you believe, you will too.

Happy Thanksgiving.

Cory
