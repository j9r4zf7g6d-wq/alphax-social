Last week we talked about the labels the world handed us. This week we go one layer deeper, because some of the heaviest labels did not come from the world. They came from the very place that was supposed to be safe.

If you have ever been hurt by a church, a pastor, a ministry, or people who said they were following God, this post is for you.

## It was real, and it still hurts

Maybe someone judged your past before they ever learned your name. Maybe you asked for help and got a lecture. Maybe you were welcomed on Sunday and quietly avoided by Wednesday. Maybe you told the truth about where you had been, and the room got colder.

That hurt is real. You do not have to talk yourself out of it, and nobody gets to tell you it was small.

## Two things that got welded together

Here is what happens. The people who hurt us were standing in a building with God's name on the door. So in our hearts, the people and God got welded into one thing. When they let us down, it felt like God let us down. When they walked away, it felt like He did.

But look at the two lists side by side.

People can be inconsistent. God is faithful.
People can walk away. God stays.
People can say one thing and live another. God means what He says.

The people who failed us were fallible, and so are we. Every one of us. Putting our full weight on a person, a program, or a building was always going to crack. That is not an excuse for what they did. It is simply a reason to put our faith back where it belongs: in Him, not in the room.

## Don't let it cost you twice

The first loss was what happened. The second loss is quieter. It is walking away from the one source of hope that never failed us because someone else failed us.

You were hurt once. You do not have to be robbed twice.

## This is the Identity 2.0 part

In the first post we asked, who did the world say we are? Here is the next question: who are we when someone who should have known better got it wrong?

We are more than what happened to us. The worst moment, the cold shoulder, the whispered story, the closed door. None of it is your name. You were made on purpose and loved before anyone had an opinion about you. Identity is not earned in a church lobby, and it cannot be revoked there either. It is received.

## Where to start (no building required)

Reconciliation is not a deadline. It is not pretending nothing happened. It is not walking into a building before you are ready. And it is not blaming yourself.

It can start much smaller than that:

1. Tell God where it hurt. Plainly, in your own words, in your own room. He is not keeping score.
2. Separate the list. Write down what people did, and next to it, who God is. Keep them in two columns.
3. Go at your own pace. Reconnecting with people, or with a church, comes when you are ready, not when someone else says it is time.
4. Find one safe person. Not a crowd. One person who listens without a lecture.

You do not have to walk into a church today. Start right where you are.

## A last word

If you are carrying a church wound, we are not going to rush you, and we are not going to tell you it is nothing. We are going to tell you it was never the whole story. You are more than what happened to you, and the God you thought left is closer than you think.
